The C500 features an 800 W air-cooled spindle, ER11 collet, with a max speed of 18,000 RPM.

The machine ships with a 6 mm spindle collet by default. Four collet specifications are supported — Φ3 mm, Φ3.15 mm, Φ6 mm and Φ6.35 mm — to match different end mill shanks.

Milling cutters are available in four shank diameters: Φ3 mm, Φ3.15 mm, Φ6 mm and Φ6.35 mm, with tool sleeves and collets provided in four matching specifications. An milling cutter is press-fitted into a tool sleeve, then mounted into the collet matching its shank for spindle cutting; the tool sleeve installation and removal tool is compatible with all four sizes.

###### Q: What is the machine's precision? Can the precision be better than ±0.02mm?

The machine can consistently maintain a tolerance of ±0.05 mm. Under ideal conditions, its theoretical accuracy can reach ±0.02 mm, based on test cutting in aluminum alloy.
The 0.02mm is an estimated average. In aluminum-alloy test cutting, the machine has achieved an accuracy of 0.01 mm. We recommend using coolant and selecting the right endmill to ensure the best accuracy.

###### Q: Does the C500 have CE certification?

# Product Use

###### Q: What scenarios is the C500 designed for?

The C500 is designed for CNC milling in a controlled indoor environment. Applications include creative design and part machining by makers, DIY enthusiasts, independent designers, and prototype developers; teaching, practical training, and competition training at universities and vocational schools; prototyping and small-batch production of precision parts and fixtures by small and medium-sized businesses, startups, and R&D organizations; and precision machining for PCB fabrication, precision molds, jewelry, and medical-device accessories.
The machine must not be used for mass industrial production, in areas requiring explosion-proof equipment, for machining parts used in special equipment, or for machining confidential military components. It must not be used to machine medical implants or life-support components.

###### Q: What are the operating environment requirements of the C500?

The machine is intended for dry indoor environments only. The environment must be free of explosion hazards, highly corrosive gases, and conductive dust.

The operating environment must meet the following requirements:

| Environmental Factor  | Requirement                 |
| :-------------------- | :-------------------------- |
| Operating Temperature | 5°C~50°C (41°F~122°F)       |
| Storage Temperature   | −20°C~70°C (−4°F~158°F)     |
| Relative Humidity     | 20%~90% RH, no condensation |
| Altitude              | ≤1829 m (6000 ft)           |

###### Q: What are the worktable load requirements of the C500?

The worktable must have a rated load capacity of at least 250 kg and be able to withstand the dynamic loads generated during operation. A heavy-duty cast-iron worktable, a steel-welded worktable, or any platform with sufficient strength and rigidity is recommended. Mobile worktables with casters must have reliable locking and stay locked during operation.

###### Q: What are the power installation requirements of the C500?

Before connecting the machine to a power supply, make sure the electrical circuit meets the following requirements:

| Item                        | Requirement                                                  |
| :-------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Input Voltage               | 110~240 VAC                                                  |
| Frequency                   | 50 / 60 Hz                                                   |
| Total Power                 | 1.6 kW                                                       |

If the equipment is moved from a cold environment to a warm one, do not power it on immediately; let it stand for 4 ~ 6 hours until condensation has fully evaporated. Power sockets without grounding or with poor grounding are strictly prohibited.

###### Q: What are the compressed air requirements of the C500?

The Air (external air supply) port must be connected to a stable air source with a rated pressure of 0.6 MPa and a maximum of 0.8 MPa. Compressed air mainly provides chip blowing and cooling to protect machining quality and reduce tool wear. The air compressor is powered by 220 V AC and is ready for use once the pressure gauge reads 0.8 MPa.

###### Q: What are the coolant requirements of the C500?

Pure water is recommended as the equipment coolant. When using the tank and spray system, clean tap water or a dedicated coolant can be filled in; a dedicated coolant is recommended for metal machining, and the liquid level must stay within the tank scale marks. When using vegetable-oil-based, mineral-oil-based, fully synthetic or semi-synthetic water-based coolants, choose the type according to the workpiece material and strictly follow the product instructions, dilution ratio and usage rules. Clean residual liquid promptly after use and apply rust protection to the metal surfaces.

###### Q: What smart features does the C500 offer?

The machine features a 3D probe, automatic tool setting (± 0.005 mm accuracy) and smart anti-collision, built on a full closed-loop servo control system and a safety interlock system. The right-side camera monitors the machining process in real time to help prevent accidents.

###### Q: What is RFID tool management on the C500?

Each tool has its own RFID tag ("one tool, one tag") that stores and updates information such as tool specifications, model, usage count and calibration date. The built-in RFID reader automatically reads the tag data, enabling automatic tool recognition, automatic tool change and full tool lifecycle management.

###### Q: How do I connect the C500 to a PC or network?

**Wired**: connect the PC and the machine with a USB cable, then read the IP address and pairing code from the machine screen and enter them in NestStudio. 
**Wireless**: put the machine and the software on the same Wi-Fi network and enter the IP address and pairing code shown on the machine screen into NestStudio. 
The machine can also be connected to the network via the RJ45 port for remote monitoring, program transfer, device management and production data collection. No internet connection is required for normal operation; the network is only used for software and firmware updates, accessing user cases, and signing in to a WorksBase community account.

###### Q: Can the C500 be operated manually?

The C500 can be used offline with third-party control software and can also be operated manually like a conventional milling machine.

###### Q: How do I perform tool offset without ATC?

The tool setter is independent of the ATC. Even without ATC, the tool setter is available for you to set tool lengths after each manual tool change to avoid crashes.

###### Q: What materials can the C500 CNC machine process?

C500 supports machining a wide range of common materials, including stainless steel, titanium alloys, aluminum, brass, steel, plastics, acrylic, wood, epoxy sheets, and fiberglass sheets. It is suitable for makers, engineers, and small studios for prototyping, custom parts, and functional component machining.

###### Q: What materials are prohibited from being machined on the C500?

Do not machine high-hardness brittle materials such as ceramics or cemented carbide, or flammable, explosive or self-igniting materials such as magnesium alloys or zinc-magnesium alloys;
Do not machine hazardous materials such as asbestos, cyanide-containing substances or highly toxic volatiles. When machining carbon fiber, epoxy resin or similar materials, the dust extraction system must be turned on and professional respiratory protection must be worn;
Do not use tools beyond the spindle specification or tool magazine compatibility, or damaged, deformed or non-standard tools.

###### Q: When centering a workpiece, should the red dot align with the tool center or the workpiece center? Do I need to adjust the red dot position manually?

According to the current internal guidance, workpiece centering should be performed by aligning the camera center with the center of the stock, rather than using the red laser dot as the centering reference.The red dot comes from the laser distance sensor and is mainly used to support surface-distance measurement.
During the automatic probing/centering process, the laser distance sensor will move automatically to measure the stock surface, so you do not need to manually adjust the red dot or make sure it coincides with either the tool center or the workpiece center.

###### Q: What is laser edge tracing? When should I use it? Does it cut the workpiece?

Laser edge tracing is a pre-machining verification feature. It checks for machining offset errors in advance, preventing failures caused by improper clamping of the blank and helping you complete precise positioning and calibration before machining.<br/>Use it before machining, especially when the blank may not be clamped precisely. Edge tracing verifies offset errors first, so you can calibrate positioning accurately and reduce the risk of machining failures.<br/>Edge tracing is for positioning preview only — it never cuts the workpiece, so you can safely use it for pre-machining verification.

###### Q: What should I do if the traced outline is offset?

Adjust the clamping position of the workpiece promptly, then run edge tracing again to confirm the outline is properly aligned before you start machining.

###### Q: The C500 Front Chip Auger makes a noticeable noise while running. How can I tell whether this is normal, and how should it be checked?

The Front Chip Auger normally sounds different from the augers on the two sides. A difference in operating sound alone does not indicate a malfunction.
If the front auger starts normally, continues to run properly, and performs chip removal as expected, a difference in operating sound can generally be considered a normal characteristic.
If there is an unusual mechanical noise, a noticeable increase in noise, or other symptoms such as the auger failing to rotate or becoming stuck, please record a video and the operating conditions and contact support for further investigation.

###### Q: How do I install the 4th-Axis?

**Preparation**: 4th-Axis unit × 1, tailstock center × 1, Φ6 × 20 locating pins × 2, M6 × 20 hex socket screws × 4, M6 × 18 hex socket screws × 6, M6 sliding nuts × 4, and a 5 mm hex key. Make sure the worktable mounting surface is clean and level, and the machine is powered off or in E-Stop status.<br/>**Steps**: 

1. Place the tailstock on the base plate with the live center pointing toward the 4th-axis chuck, and pre-fasten it with 4 pcs of M6 × 20 hex socket screws and 4 pcs of M6 sliding nuts.
2. Lightly press the Φ6 × 20 locating pins into the corresponding locating holes on the worktable, then align the locating holes on the 4th-Axis base plate with the pins to determine the installation position (the 4th-Axis threaded hole markings on the worktable can be used as references).
3. Insert 6 pcs of M6 × 18 hex socket screws through the mounting holes on the base plate and tighten them with the 5 mm hex key.
4. With the machine powered off, locate the 4th-Axis/vise aviation connector socket inside the machining chamber, align the keying positions, connect the 4th-Axis aviation connector, and hand-tighten the locking nut.
5. Power on the machine or release the E-Stop, close the protective cover, and click Home Reset. After homing completes, gently rotate the chuck by hand to verify that it is securely mounted with no noticeable backlash — installation is then complete.

###### Q: What precautions apply when installing the 4th-Axis?

1.The 4th-Axis hardware does not support hot plugging. Never plug or unplug the aviation connector while the machine is powered on; power off the machine before installation or removal, as hot plugging may damage internal circuits.<br/>2.The 4th-Axis must be installed using the corresponding threaded holes and locating pin holes on the worktable or adapter plate.<br/>3. Do not add extra nuts in the T-slots of the worktable to secure the 4th-Axis — incorrect installation positions may cause collisions and damage the machine.

###### Q: What should I do if the machine does not detect the 4th-Axis after installation?

The machine automatically detects the 4th-Axis during the power-on self-check; normally the NestPad interface shows the 4th-Axis control function and displays “4th axis connected.” If it is not displayed, check whether the wiring and 4th-Axis firmware are normal. Accessory firmware (including the 4th-Axis, MPG handwheel, tool magazine, and electric vise) can also be updated in the system settings.

###### Q: How long a workpiece can the 4th-Axis machine, and what placement options are there?

The machinable length depends on the tool magazine placement and whether the tailstock center is used :
1.Tool magazine placed along the Y-axis (transversely), with tailstock center: with Φ80 mm stock clamped, the maximum machinable diameter is Φ100 mm and the machinable length along the X-axis is 175 mm;<br/>2.Tool magazine on the X-axis right side, with tailstock center: the machinable length along the X-axis is 70 mm (the most common installation method);<br/>3.Tool magazine on the X-axis right side, without tailstock center: a slightly longer machining range — for stock diameters of Φ60–Φ100 mm the machinable X length is 103 mm; for stock under Φ60 mm it is 130 mm.<br/>Note that removing the tailstock center increases the risk of workpiece deformation.

If an even longer machining range is required, position the tool magazine transversely and use the extension plate.
For details, see the[Technical-specifications](https://docs.worksbase.com/en/c500/getting-started/technical-specifications).

###### Q: What is the maximum workpiece diameter in 4-axis mode?

The 4-axis clamping space is Φ80 mm. When tool protrusion, base plate clearance, and safety clearance requirements are met, the maximum machinable diameter is Φ100 mm. 

###### Q: How do I clamp a workpiece on the 4th-Axis?

1. Use the supplied wrench to loosen the chuck and place the workpiece blank into the chuck.
2. Tighten the chuck with the wrench to securely clamp and fix the blank.
3. After the blank is secured, move the tailstock center to the opposite end of the workpiece for support and tighten the tailstock screw to complete clamping.

###### Q: How do I replace the chuck jaws (forward/reverse)?

Depending on workpiece size and clamping method, you can switch between forward and reverse jaws. Always install the jaws strictly in numbered order: 

1. Connect the 4th-Axis to the machine and perform homing, then rotate the jaws to a 45° angle from the horizontal plane.
2. Disconnect the 4th-Axis from the machine, loosen the jaws with the chuck wrench until they exit the chuck body, and keep the removed jaws safe.
3. Match each jaw to the numbered slot (1, 2, 3, 4) in the chuck body.
4. Strictly following the 1-to-4 order, use the chuck wrench to turn the internal scroll plate so that its first tooth engages the first tooth of the corresponding numbered jaw.
5. Keep tightening with the wrench until the jaws close.

###### Q: Can I clamp irregular workpieces on the 4th-Axis?

No. Do not use the self-centering chuck to clamp irregular workpieces — uneven force on the jaws may cause deformation or jamming. 

###### Q: How do I use 4th-Axis mode in the PC software?

Once the software is connected and the 4th-Axis is recognized, you can run A-axis homing separately from the control console. In 4th-Axis mode you can import 3D models for rotary machining: besides the basic stock dimensions, you can switch the stock type between Round and Square (Round: length L and diameter D; Square: L-X, W-Y, H-Z) and set rotary machining parameters.<br/>Common toolpath settings include material size, stock & model alignment, and tool parameters (plunge angle, safe height, spindle speed, feed rate, plunge rate, cut depth per pass, and step over). After setting the parameters, generate the toolpath and check the file information on the Preview page.<br/>**Tips**: <br/>1.After setting the alignment mode, make sure the entire model stays within the stock boundary — otherwise an overcut error may occur when generating the toolpath.<br/>2.Use Center Alignment for batch machining of long bar stock; use Left or Right Alignment for single-side machining to reserve clamping space.<br/>3.At least one tool must be installed to generate a relief toolpath; using separate tools for roughing and finishing gives better results.

###### Q: How does A-axis homing work, and what if the axis alarms?

During homing, the machine first homes the X, Y, and Z axes, then the A axis: the A axis rotates quickly in one direction and stops after triggering the limit switch, then rotates in the other direction and slowly moves back toward the home direction until the limit switch is triggered again, completing homing. If the A axis keeps rotating in the same direction, then stops and alarms, check whether the A-axis limit switch is normal.

###### Q: What 4th-Axis controls does NestPad provide?

Once the module is installed and recognized, the interface displays “4th axis connected,” and the spindle motion panel enables manual A-axis control — rotate the 4th-Axis with the A+ and A− buttons. The coordinate system settings page shows the machine and work coordinate systems and the current work origin, and the 4th-Axis Homing button automatically rotates the axis to the preset work zero position.

###### Q: How should I maintain the 4th-Axis chuck?

Plan chuck maintenance based on usage:<br/>More than 10 hours of daily operation: chuck maintenance every 7 days;<br/>Less than 10 hours per day or intermittent use: every 15–30 days;<br/>Long-term storage: complete a full maintenance procedure, then seal and store the chuck in its packaging box.<br/>**Maintenance steps**:

1. Use an air gun or soft brush to remove chips and coolant residue from the chuck body, guide surfaces, and jaw gaps.
2. Spray anti-rust oil evenly into the chuck interior and onto the guideway surfaces until a small amount of oil overflows, fully coating the chuck and jaw surfaces.
3. Inject grease through the chuck lubrication port until fresh grease overflows from the inner jaw holes. 

**Note**: never rinse the inside of the chuck directly with water — this reduces internal lubrication and rust prevention. Clean and apply rust prevention after use.

## Laser Module

###### Q: What is the C500 laser module, and what is it used for?

The C500 supports an optional 5 W laser module for laser engraving. It mounts on the spindle laser mounting rail, giving the machine laser engraving capability in addition to milling.

###### Q: What comes in the laser module package?

###### Q: What materials can the laser module process?

It is suitable for materials such as solid wood, bamboo, leather, cardboard, and coated metal.

**Note**: PVC, vinyl, chlorine-containing plastics, and materials with unknown ingredients are strictly prohibited — see the Safety section.

###### Q: Which materials must never be laser-processed?

Do not cut or engrave PVC, vinyl, or chlorine-containing plastics — these materials release toxic and corrosive gases, and any resulting machine damage is not covered by the warranty. Also avoid processing materials with unknown ingredients to prevent harmful fumes or hazardous gases. For details, see the [safety](https://docs.worksbase.com/en/safety).

###### Q: What is the laser port on the machine for?

The laser port is a reserved expansion port for external laser modules, supporting laser marking, engraving, and positioning functions to enrich processing scenarios.

###### Q: How do I install the laser module?

Follow these steps:

1. Hold both sides of the laser module with both hands and press the quick-release button on the front of the module with your thumb.
2. While holding the quick-release button, align the laser dovetail slot with the spindle laser mounting rail. Slide the module along the rail until it is fully seated, then release the button.
3. Gently shake the laser module to make sure it is securely installed.
4. Take the connection cable from the left side of the laser module, align the connector with the socket on the left side of the spindle, and insert it after confirming proper alignment.

###### Q: How do I remove the laser module?

Follow these steps:

1. Hold the front section of the aviation connector on the left side of the spindle, then pull out the laser module connector.
2. Hold both sides of the laser module, press the quick-release button on the front with your thumb, and slide the module forward along the mounting rail to remove it.

###### Q: How do I set up laser engraving?

Follow these steps:

1. Power on the machine and enter the system interface. Set parameters such as laser power, focal length, and engraving speed according to the material and processing requirements.
2. Manually adjust the laser module position to align the laser focus with the workpiece surface.
3. After adjustment, perform a small-area test engraving to check engraving clarity and depth before starting formal machining.

###### Q: Under what conditions can the laser operate?

The laser operates in an enclosed safety cover — the protective door must be closed and locked before processing. If the door cannot be fully closed, the operator must wear laser safety goggles matched to the laser wavelength. Never operate the machine if any protective parts are missing.

###### Q: What safety rules apply during laser processing?

1.Stay with the machine for the entire process; never leave it running unattended.<br/>2.Turn on the exhaust or air purification system and make sure the work area is well ventilated.<br/>3.Keep the chip outlet cover closed during laser processing.

###### Q: What happens to the laser if the protective door is opened or the E-stop is pressed?

Opening the protective door triggers the door status detection device, and the system turns off the laser output to prevent personal injury from laser leakage.<br/>Pressing the E-stop button immediately cuts power to the moving parts, stops all axis motion, turns off the laser output, and triggers an alarm.

###### Q: Can I look directly into the laser emission port?

 Never. This machine is a Class 4 laser product. The laser module generates high temperatures and high-intensity laser radiation during operation — do not look directly into the laser emission port, avoid eye or skin exposure to direct or scattered laser radiation, and always wear dedicated laser safety goggles during operation.

###### Q: What should I do if the laser fails to start or the output is abnormal?

If the laser module fails to start, shows abnormal laser output, or has poor electrical contact, stop the machine and disconnect the power immediately, then contact professional personnel for maintenance.

###### Q: How should I clean and maintain the laser module?

1.Before use, make sure the laser module is mounted securely with no looseness or deviation, so machine vibration does not affect engraving precision.<br/>2.Clean the focusing lens surface regularly with the dedicated cleaning cloth; never scrape the lens with hard objects.<br/>3.Turn off the machine power when the laser module will not be used for an extended period, and avoid impact or dropping during transportation.
